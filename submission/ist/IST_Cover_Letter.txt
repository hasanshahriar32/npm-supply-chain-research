Dear Editor,

We are submitting “Maintainer Prioritization in npm Supply Chains: Objectives, Representation Sensitivity, and Metadata Limits” for consideration as a research article in Information and Software Technology.

When a team changes the npm accounts it reviews, it may bring some applications into scope and drop others. We test whether preserving package versions in the dependency graph changes that list at a fixed review budget. We select the accounts before gathering the consumer evidence, then check coverage against project manifests, committed lockfiles, and registry records for the locked versions.

In a held-out set of 215 YunoHost applications, the two five-account portfolios reach different applications in five confirmed cases. A team switching to the version-based selection would add two applications and lose three among those cases. The documented turnover bounds are 2.3% to 26.0%. In a separate 155-project Awesome-Selfhosted frame, follow-up checks resolve 88 pairs and bound turnover at 0% to 43.2%. These results show why a fixed-budget review needs to compare the application lists and keep unresolved evidence visible. The replication companion reconstructs the paired outcomes and links each confirmed switch to pinned source files.

For readers who use dependency graphs to plan software supply-chain reviews, the study shows how to check whether a ranking change affects the applications they need to examine.
