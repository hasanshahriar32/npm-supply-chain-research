TITLE
Maintainer Prioritization in npm Supply Chains: Objectives, Representation Sensitivity, and Metadata Limits

ABSTRACT
\textbf{Context.} Software supply-chain review teams have limited account budgets. Dependency graph representations can affect which applications are covered by the selected accounts.

\textbf{Objective.} We measure whether name-based and version-continuous rankings change documented application coverage at equal account budgets.

\textbf{Method.} We freeze two five-account portfolios on an npm training graph and check committed dependency paths in two held-out application frames. We use exact-version registry observations to link the paths to selected accounts and bound turnover from unresolved evidence. We also compare graph objectives and conduct an exploratory budget check.

\textbf{Results.} The 215-project YunoHost frame contains five confirmed coverage changes, with three applications reached only by name selection and two only by version selection. Follow-up checks resolve 164 pairs. Turnover lies between 2.3% and 26.0%, and the net direction remains unresolved. In the self-hosted frame, follow-up resolves 88 of 155 pairs with no confirmed change. At 50 accounts, the exploratory YunoHost check finds one confirmed difference. In the training graph at budget 100, greedy reaches 5,538 repositories and exact-union ranking reaches 4,407. Their modeled exposure reductions are 11.48% and 33.79%, respectively.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Graph representation can change which applications enter review scope at the same budget. Pinned consumer evidence identifies changes and quantifies the uncertainty in aggregate coverage.
