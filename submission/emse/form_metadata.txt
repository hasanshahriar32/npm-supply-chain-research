TITLE
Maintainer Prioritization in npm Supply Chains: Objectives, Representation Sensitivity, and Metadata Limits

ABSTRACT
\textbf{Context.} Software supply-chain review teams have limited account budgets. Dependency graph representations can affect which applications are covered by the selected accounts.

\textbf{Objective.} We measure whether name-based and version-continuous rankings change documented application coverage at equal account budgets.

\textbf{Method.} We freeze two five-account portfolios on an npm training graph and check committed dependency paths in two held-out application frames. We link those paths to selected accounts through exact-version registry observations. We bound turnover, the share of applications covered by exactly one portfolio, while retaining unresolved cases. We also compare graph objectives and examine other budgets.

\textbf{Results.} The YunoHost comparison confirms five switches. The name portfolio alone reaches three applications and the version portfolio alone reaches two. With follow-up checks, 164 of 215 pairs are resolved and turnover lies between 2.3% and 26.0%. In Awesome-Selfhosted, 88 of 155 pairs are resolved, with no confirmed switches and turnover bounded at 0–43.2%. The net direction remains unresolved in both frames. An exploratory YunoHost check finds a separate switch at 50 accounts. On the original graph at budget 100, greedy coverage reaches 5,538 repositories and exact union reaches 4,407, with modeled exposure reductions of 11.48% and 33.79%, respectively.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Graph representation can change which applications enter review scope at the same budget. Pinned consumer evidence identifies changes and quantifies the uncertainty in aggregate coverage.
