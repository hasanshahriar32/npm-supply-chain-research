## Novelty and journal fit

Concentrated npm maintainer reach already has a direct empirical precedent. Coordinated upstream selection and its downstream consequences also have recent precedent. The manuscript's distinct contribution is the fixed-budget comparison with paired observations from held-out consumers, including explicit bounds when identity or paths cannot be resolved. Put this question among the stated research questions and give it a primary results subsection. Move the detailed importer and release diagnostics to the supplement, retaining the strongest positive case and the limiting case in the main text. [Zimmermann et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/zimmerman), [He et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/pinning-is-futile-you-need-more-than-local-dependency-versioning-to-defend-against-supply-chain-attacks/).

EMSE emphasizes applied empirical work with practical relevance. One recent EMSE lockfile paper uses developer interviews to support conclusions about developer experience. A recent Information and Software Technology article compares platform dependency records with project files to evaluate record accuracy. For this paper, a commit-pinned consumer lockfile supports a claim about recorded dependency reach. A checksum-matched release supports a claim about shipped files. Actual improvements in security or organizational review efficiency would require measured outcomes from that setting. [EMSE scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/10664/aims-and-scope), [Gamage et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-025-10789-w), [Bifolco et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2025.107854).
