\section{Introduction}
A software supply-chain review team with a fixed account budget needs to know which downstream applications its selection reaches. Merging package versions by name can create paths that disappear when the graph preserves exact-version continuity. Changing the representation can therefore change the accounts selected and the applications reached. Prior research measures upstream npm leverage and coordinated dependency changes~\cite{samaana2025,he2025}. We evaluate the coverage consequences of that choice using pinned application lockfiles and exact-version registry observations.

The decision also depends on the objective. A team may seek to reach more repositories, account for more of the modeled exposure, or select every observed source associated with a repository. Two accounts can reach many of the same repositories and still contribute separate account--repository pairs. We measure these tradeoffs in the graph and evaluate the representation choice using a separate set of application-owned records.

The original graph comes from an expansion seeded by high-impact npm packages. It contains 25,228 identifiers, including bundled contexts, and 12,217 observed usernames. We evaluate reach over 7,171 normalized repository keys associated with observed workflow directories. We compare ranking and greedy-coverage baselines, weighted combinations of objectives, and a heuristic that prioritizes all-source exclusion.

We vary the heuristic's components and compare its results with exact optima on restricted candidate sets. Fresh dependency, maintainer and workflow observations from 100 sampled roots let us check which findings repeat in a bounded cohort. We also use a stratified probability sample of 120 endpoints to characterize commit-pinned evidence for repository mappings and workflow files. The endpoint indicators are automated and lack manual validation.

We ask five questions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How large are the observed coverage, additive-exposure and all-source-exclusion tradeoffs at equal budgets?
\item \textbf{RQ2:} How do representation choices affect evaluation, and what evidence supports the workflow-associated endpoint observations?
\item \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive is the exclusion-oriented heuristic to its components, candidate cap and ties, and where does it miss candidate-restricted optima?
\item \textbf{RQ4:} Which tradeoffs persist in a separately collected cohort and among endpoints and accounts observed in both datasets?
\item \textbf{RQ5:} At the same account budget, which held-out applications change documented coverage with the graph representation, and how much uncertainty remains?
\end{itemize}

Our main contribution is a paired comparison of documented downstream coverage under a fixed account budget. We freeze the five-account name and version portfolios before collecting evidence from Awesome-Selfhosted and YunoHost. Five YunoHost applications have confirmed switches, and we bound turnover over the full frames, including unresolved cases. The graph experiments show how much distinct coverage an additive ranking trades for modeled exposure reduction. The heuristic comparisons show where the extra scoring helps and where restricted exact benchmarks expose gaps. Together, these results show which conclusions depend on the selection objective, graph representation, or available evidence.
