\section{Pareto Optimization and Heterogeneous Risk: RQ4}
\label{sec:pareto_and_weights}

In Research Question 4, we examine whether multi-objective scalarization and heterogeneous risk formulations can overcome the coverage--exposure trade-off and target structural bottlenecks that unweighted heuristics miss.

\subsection{Multi-Objective Pareto Scalarization ($J_\alpha$)}
To discover the Pareto frontier between submodular coverage $C(S)$ and modular scenario exposure reduction $H(S)$, we evaluated the hybrid objective $J_\alpha(S) = \alpha \frac{C(S)}{|R_{\text{wf}}|} + (1-\alpha) H(S)$ across scalarization weights $\alpha \in \{0.00, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, 1.00\}$ at budget $|S|=100$.

Table~\ref{tab:q1_pareto_frontier} presents the Pareto frontier outcomes across both package-projected and version-continuous graphs, and Fig.~\ref{fig:q1_pareto_frontier} depicts the empirical trade-off surface.

The empirical Pareto frontier reveals a pronounced knee region at \textbf{$\alpha = 0.50$}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On the package-projected graph, $\alpha=0.50$ achieves $C(S) = 5{,}020$ repositories ($70.00\%$) and $H(S) = 31.19\%$ exposure reduction, simultaneously securing $P(S) = 626$ fully excluded repositories.
    \item On the version-continuous graph, $\alpha=0.50$ achieves $C(S) = 5{,}000$ repositories ($69.73\%$) and $H(S) = 38.65\%$ exposure reduction ($P=648$).
\end{itemize}

Compared to pure Greedy Coverage ($\alpha=1.00$), shifting to $\alpha=0.50$ incurs a minor coverage penalty of only $-7.23$ percentage points ($77.23\% \rightarrow 70.00\%$), while delivering a massive gain of \textbf{$+19.71$ percentage points} in exposure reduction ($11.48\% \rightarrow 31.19\%$, a $2.72\times$ increase). Compared to pure Exact Union ($\alpha=0.00$), $\alpha=0.50$ sacrifices only $-2.60$ percentage points of exposure reduction ($33.79\% \rightarrow 31.19\%$) while capturing $+613$ additional covered repositories ($+8.54$ percentage points). Thus, equal-weight scalarization resolves the single-objective trade-off dilemma by retaining over $90\%$ of the maximum attainable coverage and exposure reduction simultaneously.

At budget $|S|=100$, the direct $P(S)$ heuristic achieves \textbf{$P(S) = 1{,}022$ fully protected repositories} ($14.25\%$ of the entire corpus). This represents an improvement of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$+222$ repositories} ($+27.75\%$ relative gain) over Greedy Coverage ($P=800$), and
    \item \textbf{$+488$ repositories} ($+91.39\%$ relative gain) over Exact Union ($P=534$).
\end{itemize}
Crucially, this supermodular gain is achieved without compromising perimeter defense: the heuristic maintains $C(S) = 5{,}020$ repositories ($70.0\%$) and $H(S) = 13.23\%$. This establishes that registry operators seeking complete repository isolation can achieve nearly double the structural protection of traditional rankings by actively prioritizing maintainers of near-isolated repositories.

\subsubsection{Account-Level Sole-Maintainer Weighting}
Under account-level sole-maintainer weighting ($w_m^{\text{sole}} = 1 + |\text{sole\_pkgs}(m)|$), weight-aware optimization ($\arg\max_S H_{\text{sole}}(S)$) eliminates \textbf{$88.04\%$} of ecosystem exposure mass at budget 100. This proves that nearly nine-tenths of solo-maintainer supply-chain exposure across 7,171 workflow repositories is concentrated within just 100 maintainer accounts ($0.819\%$ of the population).

\subsubsection{The Package-Level Sole-Maintainer Bottleneck ($H_{\text{pkg-sole}}$)}
A crucial discovery from our empirical audit is that account-level metrics severely misspecify package-level single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities. When risk weighting is applied strictly to packages having $|M(p)| = 1$ (no co-maintainers to conduct peer review), unweighted heuristics perform poorly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unweighted Greedy Coverage eliminates only \textbf{$23.40\%$} of package-level sole-maintainer exposure.
    \item Unweighted Exact Union eliminates only \textbf{$27.32\%$}.
    \item By contrast, Weight-Aware Package-Sole Optimization ($\arg\max_S H_{\text{pkg-sole}}(S)$) eliminates \textbf{$67.37\%$} of package-level sole-maintainer exposure mass.
\end{itemize}

This represents an improvement of \textbf{$+40.05$ percentage points} ($2.47\times$ higher risk reduction) at the exact same budget of 100 maintainers. Furthermore, this targeted optimization maintains high perimeter defense, covering $4{,}718$ workflow repositories ($65.79\%$) and fully excluding $642$ repositories. This structural theorem demonstrates that software supply-chain defenses must differentiate between account-level portfolio breadth and package-level co-maintenance governance: protecting accounts solely by dependency volume leaves the most fragile single-maintainer dependencies exposed.
