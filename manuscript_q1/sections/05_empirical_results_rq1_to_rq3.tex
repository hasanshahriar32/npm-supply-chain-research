At budget $|S|=100$, \textbf{Greedy Coverage} achieves $C(S) = 5{,}538$ repositories ($77.23\%$), establishing a descriptive lead of $+1{,}131$ repositories ($+15.77$ percentage points) over \textbf{Exact Union} ($C(S) = 4{,}407$, $61.46\%$). However, when evaluated under scenario-weighted additive exposure reduction $H(S)$, Exact Union achieves $33.79\%$, outpacing Greedy Coverage ($11.48\%$) by \textbf{$+22.31$ percentage points} ($2.94\times$ relative advantage).

This divergence demonstrates the primary trade-off governing supply-chain defense:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extensive Defense (Submodular Coverage):} Greedy coverage spreads interventions across the widest perimeter of consumer repositories, securing any repository having at least one top maintainer.
    \item \textbf{Intensive Defense (Modular Exposure):} Exact union prioritizes maintainers whose accounts touch large volumes of package dependencies across all repositories, eliminating total identity-exposure mass.
\end{enumerate}

Portfolio size ranking performs poorly across all objectives, achieving only $63.85\%$ coverage and $7.96\%$ exposure reduction at $|S|=100$. This confirms that raw package count is a deceptive indicator of systemic risk: maintainers with hundreds of dormant utility packages exert minimal downstream blast radius compared to maintainers of foundational packages with compact portfolios.

\subsection{RQ2: Multi-Homing Residual Exposure and Single-Source Fragility}
A central mechanism explaining the gap between $C(S)$ and $H(S)$ is \textit{multi-homing dependency}: downstream workflow repositories rely on multiple distinct maintainers simultaneously. In our corpus, consumer repositories have a mean of $3.12$ maintainer sources in their direct and transitive dependency closures, with heavy-tailed repositories depending on up to $182$ distinct maintainers.

Consequently, covering a repository under $C(S)$ does not eliminate its compromise probability. If repository $r$ depends on $\text{Sources}(r) = \{m_1, m_2, m_3, m_4\}$, hardening only $m_1$ satisfies $r \in \bigcup_{m \in S} E_m$, yet leaves 3 unmitigated compromise pathways ($75\%$ residual vulnerability under uniform compromise probability). Full immunity requires all-source exclusion $P(S)$, which is supermodular.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:multi_budget_comparison}, at $|S|=100$, Greedy Coverage achieves $P(S) = 800$ fully protected repositories ($11.16\%$), while Exact Union achieves only $P(S) = 534$ ($7.45\%$). This descriptive difference of $\Delta P = +266$ repositories occurs because greedy coverage systematically targets maintainers of single-source leaf repositories. Nonetheless, over $88\%$ of workflow repositories retain unhardened dependency paths under both baseline heuristics at budget 100.

\subsection{RQ3: Version-Continuity Representation and Selection Penalties}
Comparing the package-projected name graph against the version-continuous SemVer dependency graph reveals substantial topological distortion:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exposure Shrinkage:} Preserving version continuity contracts total maintainer-repository exposure pairs from $462{,}170$ to $266{,}016$---an exact reduction of \textbf{$42.44\%$}.
    \item \textbf{Root Scope Changes:} $8{,}587$ out of $25{,}228$ packages ($34.04\%$) exhibit divergent downstream reach scopes between name-collapsed and version-continuous representations.
    \item \textbf{Cycle Condensation:} Kosaraju condensation detects $82$ cyclic nodes across $68$ strongly connected components in the name graph, compared to $68$ cyclic components across $40{,}806$ version nodes in the version-continuous graph.
    \item \textbf{Phantom-Reach Witnesses:} We identified 5 verified packages whose name-projected reach is heavily inflated by multi-version conflation: \texttt{pseudomap}, \texttt{is}, \texttt{foreach}, \texttt{indexof}, and \texttt{has-color}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Cross-Representation Selection Penalty}
When maintainers are selected based on name-projected heuristics but evaluated on the real-world version-continuous graph, prioritization efficacy degrades substantially. For the \texttt{sum\_package\_reach} heuristic at budget 100, ranking directly on version-continuous topologies captures \textbf{$34.34\%$} of version exposure, compared to only \textbf{$20.91\%$} when using name-projected rankings---an improvement of \textbf{$+13.43$ percentage points} ($+64.2\%$ relative gain).

The case study illustrates three critical phenomena:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Phantom Inflation:} Foundational utilities exhibit massive phantom inflation under name projection: \texttt{simple-swizzle} is inflated by \textbf{$+287.1\%$} ($2{,}969$ vs. $767$ repositories), \texttt{chalk} by \textbf{$+91.7\%$} ($5{,}293$ vs. $2{,}761$), \texttt{ansi-styles} by \textbf{$+85.9\%$} ($5{,}133$ vs. $2{,}761$), and \texttt{debug} by \textbf{$+25.8\%$} ($2{,}971$ vs. $2{,}362$).
    \item \textbf{Co-Maintenance Masking:} Maintainer \texttt{qix} (author of \texttt{debug} and \texttt{simple-swizzle}) is excluded by unweighted Greedy Coverage beyond budget 100 (rank $\#>100$) because co-maintainers with broader portfolios satisfy the marginal coverage check first. Under Exact Union and Pareto Hybrid ($\alpha=0.50$), \texttt{qix} is correctly prioritized at rank \textbf{$\#8$} and \textbf{$\#4$}, respectively.
    \item \textbf{Targeted Compromise Reach:} Ecosystem reach varies by orders of magnitude: foundational packages like \texttt{chalk} reach $2{,}761$ repositories ($38.5\%$), whereas targeted attack packages like \texttt{@solana/web3.js} reach $48$ repositories, \texttt{nx} reaches $142$, and \texttt{node-ipc} reaches $18$.
\end{itemize}
