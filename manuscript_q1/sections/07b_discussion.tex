\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The choice of objective depends on the review task. $C$ counts distinct endpoints reached. $H$ measures the selected share of weighted account--repository pairs. $P$ counts endpoints whose observed sources are all selected. Each objective answers a different planning question. Estimating how many incidents a control prevents would require intervention evidence.

Exact union maximizes $H$ by construction. Its distinct coverage and all-source exclusion depend on which repositories the selected accounts share. The coverage--exposure ordering repeats in the bounded cohort. The change in pair mass under version continuity differs between the original graph and the cohort, at 42.44\% and 3.33\%, respectively. Those magnitudes reflect both the representation and the observed population.

In YunoHost, moving to the version portfolio adds two applications and drops three among the confirmed cases. The net difference among those five cases is one fewer application, even though five review decisions change. The turnover lower bound is positive for every cutoff choice. At budget 50 a different application switches coverage. The Awesome-Selfhosted follow-ups leave 67 of 155 pairs unresolved, including 28 projects without a retained root manifest. The frames have different evidence support, and their turnover bounds overlap even within the groups analyzed with npm files. Missing dependency evidence leaves the direction of aggregate net change uncertain.

For a fixed-size account review, a team can compare the applications reached by each proposed portfolio before replacing the current selection. The comparison can retain one-sided hits with an unresolved counterpart and link confirmed changes to the pinned dependency paths. For example, switching from the name to the version portfolio brings Y017 into documented scope and removes Y213 from it. Both applications warrant attention when planning the change, even if the goal is to keep the number of covered applications stable.

The recipe audit shows why a repository alone is too coarse a unit when the review starts from deployment inputs. The recipe can select a different source revision or a specific package importer within the repository. Even at the same commit, using the recipe's named frontend can change whether a dependency path is documented. For review programs that start with shipped components, the artifact itself determines what can be confirmed. A preserved dependency directory allowed us to verify file inclusion in the Mongo Express release. An identical lockfile in the Pterodactyl source and release established continuity of the dependency declarations, while the compiled assets left module membership unresolved.

Omitting reciprocal scoring increases $P$ by two endpoints on the original graph. In the bounded cohort, the original rule has six more all-source exclusions than that simpler variant. Candidate-restricted exact instances expose large deficits alongside frequent optimum matches. The value of the extra scoring varies across the two observed graphs.

The endpoint audit provides reproducible, commit-pinned evidence collection. Its automated indicators were not manually validated and do not establish mapping accuracy, workflow execution or historical validity. A deployment recommendation would additionally require observed authorization paths, intervention costs, feasible control policies and outcome evaluation. These missing measurements are separate from whether the retained graph computations are correct.
