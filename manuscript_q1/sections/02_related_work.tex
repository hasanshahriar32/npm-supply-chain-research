\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

We position the study against work on maintainer allocation, dependency structure and consumer-side measurement.

\subsection{Ecosystem Structure and Centrality-Based Prioritization}
Decan et al. measured how dependency concentration evolved across seven package ecosystems~\cite{decan2019}. In the present study, concentration motivates examining the distribution of reachable repositories across maintainer accounts. We compare three objectives at identical account budgets and measure the cost of optimizing one objective in units of another.

\subsection{Developer allocation and consumer evidence}
Schueller and Wachs model how developer departures and dependency relationships jointly affect libraries in the Rust ecosystem and compare allocations of limited developer support~\cite{schueller2024}. Hamer et al. study contributor reputation as a signal for prioritizing dependency reviews in Rust~\cite{hamer2025trust}. Our empirical question measures which consumer projects gain or lose documented coverage when dependency representations change the fixed-budget selection of accounts. Zerouali et al. distinguish dependents distributed through registries from external GitHub consumers~\cite{zerouali2022}. We draw a separate application sample because a package's development repository and an application that consumes the package supply evidence about different relationships.

Samaana et al. vary dependency depth in an npm replication and find changes in the magnitude of measured technical leverage~\cite{samaana2025}. Chinthanet et al. distinguish upstream fixing releases from downstream adoption~\cite{chinthanet2021}, and Alfadel et al. investigate what vulnerability information was discoverable at different points in time~\cite{alfadel2023}. These studies motivate preserving dependency versions, consumer identity and observation dates in each comparison. Mohayeji et al. measure how developers respond to dependency updates~\cite{mohayeji2025}. Our outcome is the presence of a documented dependency path at a pinned commit. Observing how developers act on a review recommendation would require a separate study.

Bifolco et al. measure discrepancies in dependency and dependent records in the GitHub dependency graph~\cite{bifolco2025accuracy}. Gamage et al. compare lockfile semantics and maintenance across seven package managers~\cite{gamage2026lockfiles}. Wang et al. characterize dependency semantics across package managers and reconstruct registry dependencies over time with OpenDep~\cite{wang2026opendep}. We evaluate a paired portfolio decision against paths committed by external consumers. That outcome requires identifying the consumer importer, following its version-resolved dependency paths, and observing registry account associations for the resulting versions.
