\section{Robustness and Research Implications}
\subsection{Observation and Selection Sensitivities}
We conservatively exclude 60 positive package-version observations whose stored repository key disagrees with a lexical parse of the source URL. We do not transfer a workflow flag to another repository without reacquisition. This reduces the endpoint universe by 24 repositories, from 7,171 to 7,147; greedy at 100 covers 5,524, with $P=799$. This check addresses a specific mapping defect and cannot certify the remaining remote associations.

Reversing tie order changes greedy coverage from 5,538 to 5,537 and $P$ from 800 to 793. Excluding the 1, 5, or 10 largest portfolios from selectable identities, while retaining all original source scenarios, gives coverage of 5,522, 5,149, and 5,076. These are eligibility sensitivities, without inferring whether accounts are bots or organizations. Grouping equal stored email hashes as a hypothetical control unit yields 12,147 groups and leaves greedy coverage at 5,538. Hash matches are not validated common credential control; the group-based scenario denominator also changes.

\subsection{From Structural Reach to Outcome Research}
Our findings suggest distinct uses for the quantities. A team seeking broad review coverage can use $C$; the stipulated fixed-prior account-hardening decision uses $H$. Complete modeled source exclusion requires $P$ and may demand complementary selections. Actual budgets also depend on control cost, feasibility, account-specific probabilities, publishing permissions, and adversary adaptation. Our equal-cost fixed-prior experiment does not observe these. Workflow association alone cannot establish credential-harvesting return or a control's causal effect.

We completed acquisition groundwork for prospective outcome research by sampling 100 graph identifiers uniformly without replacement from our 25,228-name frame, using seed 20261020 and freezing selection before requests. Current npm metadata were captured for 90; all eight HTTP 405 responses concern bundled-context identifiers, while two ordinary names return HTTP 404. These records remain unavailable without replacement; bundled-identifier eligibility requires resolution before outcome surveillance. The feature cutoff is 20 October 2026, 00:48:38 in Asia/Dhaka, with a 90-day horizon ending 18 January 2027 at the same local time. All 100 outcomes remain pending. This pilot demonstrates acquisition feasibility; its size does not establish adequate event yield or predictive power.

Future reported-compromise evaluation must fix surveillance sources, reporting delays, censoring, and family-grouped temporal splits before examining outcomes. An unreported package is unlabeled under the stated surveillance process, rather than certified benign. Contemporary metadata cannot be retroactively assigned to earlier attacks. We report no trained forecast, accuracy, or empirical compromise probability from this pilot.
