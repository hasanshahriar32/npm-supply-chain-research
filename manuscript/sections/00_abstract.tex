\begin{abstract}
A maintainer review can cover a downstream repository while leaving other upstream accounts outside the review. We measure this gap in an npm graph containing \StudyPackages{} identifiers and \StudyMaintainers{} maintainer usernames; 2,745 identifiers represent bundled contexts rather than separate registry packages. Five selection rules and 200 random orders are evaluated at six budgets. With 100 selected identities, greedy coverage reaches \GreedyCovered{} of \StudyWorkflowRepos{} workflow-associated repositories (\GreedyCoveragePercent{}\%); exact maintainer-union ranking reaches \UnionCovered{}. Yet exact union removes \UnionExposurePercent{}\% of account--repository exposure pairs, compared with greedy's \GreedyExposurePercent{}\%. This exposure measure assumes a uniformly chosen single-account compromise and perfect hardening of selected accounts. Greedy selects every observed source identity for only \GreedyExcluded{} repositories. Keeping version identities continuous along paths reduces exposure pairs by \VersionPairsDecreasePercent{}\%, with little change in aggregate greedy coverage. The comparison shows why a review program must specify whether it seeks distinct repositories, account-origin exposure reduction, or complete source exclusion. These graph measurements do not establish compromise probabilities or the effectiveness of deployed controls.
\end{abstract}
