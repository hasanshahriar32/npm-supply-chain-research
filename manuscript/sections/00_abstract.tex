\begin{abstract}
Dependency reach, shared maintainers, and public continuous integration workflows offer useful but incomplete views of software supply-chain exposure. Their combination into a predictive score requires internally consistent artifacts and a defensible outcome population. We audit an npm research repository containing \PackageCount{} packages and two compressed metric artifacts. Independent recomputation reproduces all seven database metric fields, yet the companion CSV disagrees on workflow reach for \DBReachPositive{} packages: only \CSVReachPositive{} have nonzero values in the CSV, compared with \DBReachPositive{} in the database. At a one-percent review budget of \ReviewBudget{} packages, their equal-weight score queues share only \ScoreQueueIntersection{} entries. Large score ties further affect queue membership. Separately, the incident companion contains \TemporalRecords{} package-event records and \TemporalRetained{} retained pre-event source objects, but supplies no cutoff-valid control population. These findings establish artifact inconsistency and evaluation ineligibility, rather than predictive accuracy. We provide an offline audit, aggregate results, and an evidence-aware protocol separating structural exposure, workflow configuration, release authority, and future documented compromise. The contribution is a reproducible measurement study and benchmark specification; no learned model or attacker-return estimate is claimed.
\end{abstract}
