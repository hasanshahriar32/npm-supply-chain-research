\section{Introduction}
Shared dependencies concentrate software supply-chain exposure in a relatively small number of upstream packages and maintainer accounts. Prior npm research demonstrates both this concentration and the potential reach of selected maintainers~\cite{zimmermann2019,zahan2022}. A registry operator or security team with a limited budget must nevertheless decide what the selection should accomplish. Reviewing distinct downstream repositories, reducing expected exposure across hypothetical account compromises, and eliminating every modeled source of exposure at a repository require different measurements.

Consider two accounts whose packages reach nearly identical downstream repositories. Selecting both can be valuable when each account represents an independent possible origin of compromise. The second account contributes little additional repository coverage, however. Conversely, a selection that broadens coverage can leave most covered repositories reachable through unselected accounts. A coverage percentage therefore needs an explicit interpretation before it can inform an account-hardening decision.

We investigate this distinction through a reproducible structural study of our npm data. We construct maintainer portfolio unions, deduplicate workflow-associated packages into repository endpoints, and evaluate established selection rules against three separately defined quantities: distinct coverage, additive scenario exposure reduction, and exclusion of all observed source identities. We then test whether the conclusions depend on collapsing package versions, counting monorepo packages separately, limiting graph depth, or changing visibility and selection assumptions.

Our study addresses three questions. \textbf{RQ1:} How do coverage-oriented and additive exposure-oriented selections differ at the same identity budget? \textbf{RQ2:} How much covered infrastructure remains reachable through unselected identities? \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive are the results to graph representation, endpoint units, and observation gaps?

The contribution is an empirical replication and extension of maintainer-reach analysis. First, we compare five deterministic methods and a 200-order random baseline at six budgets, with exact graph closures and explicit objectives. Second, we quantify workflow-repository deduplication and version continuity effects on exposure mass and coverage. Third, we provide aggregate reproducibility records, independent correctness checks, and a frozen prospective acquisition pilot for subsequent outcome research. At 100 identities, coverage greedy reaches 77.2\% of observed workflow repositories but removes 11.5\% of exposure under our equal-account model; exact-union ranking removes 33.8\%. This divergence motivates specifying the decision objective before interpreting a ranking as defensive benefit.
