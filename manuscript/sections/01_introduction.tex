\section{Introduction}
Shared dependencies concentrate npm exposure in upstream packages and maintainer accounts~\cite{zimmermann2019,zahan2022}. Limited defensive budgets require distinguishing three objectives: reviewing distinct downstream repositories, reducing exposure across hypothetical account compromises, and eliminating every observed source at a repository.

October 2026 disclosures illustrate the boundaries of account controls. npm's SlugFest weakness involved mutable repository-name trust bindings; GitHub announced expiry of unvalidated bindings on October 2~\cite{npmtrustedpub2026,npmexpiry2026}. LiteLLM's final investigation describes an exposed master key followed by CI secret theft and misuse of multiple developer tokens~\cite{litellm2026}. Its published artifacts differed from the official source tag~\cite{litellmincident2026}. Railway reported potential environment-variable exposure and temporarily paused deployments~\cite{railway2026,railwaydisclosure2026}. These contextual cases motivate authority and CI boundaries; they do not validate our graph.

Overlapping portfolios distinguish the objectives: a second account may add little coverage but still contribute a compromise-origin scenario. We compare exact portfolio unions at deduplicated workflow-repository endpoints.

We ask: \textbf{RQ1:} How do coverage and additive exposure selections differ at equal budgets? \textbf{RQ2:} How much covered infrastructure remains reachable from unselected identities? \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive are results to representation, endpoint units, and observation gaps?

We extend maintainer-reach analysis with five deterministic methods, 200 random orders, six budgets, representation sensitivities, and independent checks. Hybrid, exclusion, threshold, and efficacy sensitivities clarify tradeoffs. A frozen acquisition pilot supports future outcome research without a forecasting claim.
