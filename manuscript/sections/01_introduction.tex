\section{Introduction}
An npm repository can depend on packages linked to several maintainer accounts. Earlier studies measured account reach and metadata signals for security review~\cite{zimmermann2019,zahan2022}. Selecting accounts for distinct repository coverage can differ from selecting them for exposure across compromise origins.

Recent disclosures show the limits of account selection. SlugFest concerned mutable repository-name trust bindings~\cite{npmtrustedpub2026}; GitHub announced expiration of unvalidated bindings on October 2, 2026~\cite{npmexpiry2026}. LiteLLM reported master-key exposure, CI secret theft, and developer-token misuse~\cite{litellm2026}, with released artifacts differing from the source tag~\cite{litellmincident2026}. Railway described possible environment-variable exposure and a deployment pause~\cite{railway2026,railwaydisclosure2026}. These reports delimit our authority and workflow assumptions; they supply no graph validation.

Reviewing a second account adds little coverage when its reach overlaps the first account's. Leaving it unselected nevertheless retains another compromise origin. We measure both quantities and complete source exclusion, deduplicating packages mapped to one workflow repository.

We ask: \textbf{RQ1:} How do coverage and additive exposure selections differ at equal budgets? \textbf{RQ2:} How much covered infrastructure remains reachable from unselected identities? \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive are results to representation, endpoint units, and observation gaps?

We compare five rules and 200 random orders at six budgets, then vary representation and observation assumptions. Hybrid and exclusion selections test the tradeoff further. A frozen metadata pilot assesses acquisition feasibility for future outcome research.
