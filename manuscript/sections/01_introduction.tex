\section{Introduction}
Shared dependencies concentrate npm exposure in upstream packages and maintainer accounts~\cite{zimmermann2019,zahan2022}. Limited defensive budgets require distinguishing three objectives: reviewing distinct downstream repositories, reducing exposure across hypothetical account compromises, and eliminating every observed source at a repository.

October 2026 disclosures illustrate the boundaries of account controls. npm's SlugFest weakness involved mutable repository-name trust bindings; GitHub announced expiry of unvalidated bindings on October 2~\cite{npmtrustedpub2026,npmexpiry2026}. LiteLLM's final investigation describes an exposed master key followed by CI secret theft and misuse of multiple developer tokens~\cite{litellm2026}. Its published artifacts differed from the official source tag~\cite{litellmincident2026}. Railway reported potential environment-variable exposure and temporarily paused deployments~\cite{railway2026,railwaydisclosure2026}. These cases motivate publishing-authority and CI boundaries; they are not observations validating our historical npm graph.

Two accounts can reach nearly identical repositories: selecting the second adds little coverage but removes another account-origin scenario. Conversely, broad coverage can leave other upstream identities unhardened. We compare these objectives using exact maintainer portfolio unions and deduplicated workflow-associated repository endpoints.

We ask: \textbf{RQ1:} How do coverage and additive exposure selections differ at equal budgets? \textbf{RQ2:} How much covered infrastructure remains reachable from unselected identities? \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive are results to representation, endpoint units, and observation gaps?

Our contribution extends established maintainer-reach analysis: five deterministic methods and 200 random orders at six budgets; endpoint and version-continuity sensitivities; and independently checked reproducibility records. Additional hybrid, exclusion-oriented, threshold, and efficacy sensitivities clarify the decision tradeoff. A frozen acquisition pilot supports future outcome research, without a forecasting claim.
