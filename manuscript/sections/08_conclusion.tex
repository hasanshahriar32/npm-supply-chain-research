\section{Conclusion}
A supply-chain prioritization score is meaningful only within an identified evidence state. In this repository, independent reconstruction reproduces the database's seven metric fields, while the paired CSV disagrees on workflow reach and score for 17,809 packages. At a 253-package review budget, the score queues share 65 entries under a stated tie rule. The same underlying package population therefore does not imply the same operational review queue.

The historical companion provides a useful incident-record starting point, but its 47 package-event records and 45 retained source objects lack a cutoff-valid control population. Predictive discrimination and calibration remain unevaluated. The next empirical step is to freeze a defensible population and historical evidence, then compare transparent baselines and separate review surfaces before fitting a graph model. The present contribution is an auditable account of artifact consistency, queue sensitivity, and the evidence required to make that next study credible.
