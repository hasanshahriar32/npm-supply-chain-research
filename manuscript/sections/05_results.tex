\subsection{RQ1: Coverage and Exposure Favor Different Selections}
Table~\ref{tab:methods} reports workflow-repository outcomes at 100 identities, or 0.819\% of observed usernames. Greedy covers \GreedyCovered{} repositories (\GreedyCoveragePercent{}\%), compared with \UnionCovered{} (61.5\%) for exact-union ranking: 25.7\% more distinct repositories. Exact-union ranking instead removes \UnionExposurePercent{}\% of the stipulated scenario exposure, compared with \GreedyExposurePercent{}\% for greedy. Its advantage on $H$ follows from the objective; the experiment quantifies the resulting coverage tradeoff. Maximum per-package reach approaches its $H$ result but covers fewer repositories.

Figure~\ref{fig:budget} shows the budget curves. At 25 identities, greedy covers 4,854 repositories, versus 4,284 for exact union and 4,067 for portfolio size. Their $H$ values are 6.08\%, 9.86\%, and 4.38\%. At 100, random mean coverage is 1,680.6, with a central 95\% simulation range of 659.8--2,610.7. The exact finite-population random expectation is 1,716.8, and expected $H$ is $100/12{,}217=0.819\%$. Simulated and exact means differ through random-order variation, rather than a sampling inference about npm.



\subsection{RQ2: Covered Endpoints Retain Other Sources}
Figure~\ref{fig:partition} partitions source-reachable repositories at budget 100. Of the 5,538 repositories covered by greedy, only 800 meet all-source exclusion; 4,738 remain reachable from an unselected identity. Across the 7,158 repositories with nonempty observed source sets, 6,358 retain such a source. Exact-union ranking excludes every observed source for 534 repositories. We make no claim that either selection optimizes $P$.

Cross-identity overlap explains the difference between broad coverage and additive scenario mass. At 100 identities, $1-C(S)/\sum_{m\in S}|E_m|$ is 89.57\% for greedy and 97.18\% for exact union. Repeated endpoints contribute to different possible account-loss scenarios in $H$, while contributing once in $C$. Such repetition is not an error for additive exposure. Reporting covered endpoints as universally protected would, however, overstate what a selected account control accomplishes.

\subsection{RQ3: Representation Changes Exposure Mass}
Preserving version continuity changes downstream closures for \ChangedRoots{} of 25,228 package roots and removes 1,273,291 ordered root--dependent pairs introduced by package projection. Maintainer workflow-repository unions change for 2,582 usernames. Total identity--repository pairs fall from 462,170 to 266,016, a \VersionPairsDecreasePercent{}\% decrease. This is representational sensitivity, not an estimate of false exploit paths in actual installations.

At 100 identities, selecting and evaluating greedy on the version graph covers 5,516 repositories, with $H=13.10\%$ and $P=817$. Exact-union ranking gives $C=4,479$, $H=40.59\%$, and $P=527$. Evaluating the package-selected greedy identities on that version graph yields $C=5,491$. Aggregate coverage remains similar at this budget, while additive exposure mass is substantially representation-dependent (Fig.~\ref{fig:representation}).

Repository deduplication affects decisions. Selecting greedy for all-package coverage, then evaluating repository endpoints, covers 5,308 repositories; selecting for workflow-package coverage gives 5,315. Direct repository selection gives 5,538. Depth ablation gives 5,255 when selection uses portfolio endpoints alone and 5,488 with one-hop evidence, both evaluated against full reach. Endpoint units and depth therefore matter with the final evaluation denominator held fixed.
