\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Maintainer prioritization.} Our reach sets and coverage baseline build on Zimmermann et al.'s maintainer analysis and vetting proposal~\cite{zimmermann2019}. Zahan et al.~\cite{zahan2022} identify metadata review signals; Wang~\cite{wang2026} simulates package-node hardening against cascades. We instead select usernames, removing their account-origin scenarios while retaining other origins' paths. The greedy coverage guarantee is established~\cite{nemhauser1978}; exact-union ranking maximizes additive exposure under equal weights and costs.

\textbf{Dependency semantics.} Dependency-tree propagation~\cite{liu2022} and version-pinning defenses~\cite{he2025} motivate resolution-aware measurement. Pillar's October 19 OpenCode disclosure concerned Windows/WSL command injection through an older npm \texttt{open} dependency, fixed September 28 without a CVE~\cite{opencode2026}. Shen et al.~\cite{gao2025emse} report 81.26\% false positives for package-level findings under finer analysis in their corpus, not npm as a whole. Jayasuriya et al.~\cite{jayasuriya2024} examine Maven breaking changes. Our version sensitivity checks path continuity without establishing vulnerable-code execution.

\textbf{Workflow and authority evidence.} Studies of workflow security~\cite{koishybayev2022}, taint analysis in ARGUS~\cite{muralee2023}, and unused CI dependencies~\cite{weeraddana2024} examine evidence beyond a directory-presence flag. Signing interviews also document obstacles to putting controls into practice~\cite{okafor2025}. Work on repository accessibility~\cite{tsakpinis2024}, registry tracking~\cite{pinckney2023}, and release-point reconstruction~\cite{rahman2026} informs our treatment of missing and time-dependent observations. Our contribution is the measured comparison of selection objectives on the retained graph.
