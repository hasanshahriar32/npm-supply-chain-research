\section{Validity, Limitations, and Responsible Reporting}
\textbf{Constructs.} Maintainer membership does not establish publishing authority, account independence, token scope, or control feasibility. Reach does not prove installation, CI execution, or secret access. Uniform priors, perfect hardening, $\eta$, and threshold rules are stipulated scenarios. Token roles and package-level signer groups are unobserved; residual thresholds cannot guarantee operational quorum protection.

\textbf{Observation and generalization.} Collection lacks a common field-level cutoff, workflow commit identities, and extraction-root provenance. Both graphs mix resolution contexts. Missing metadata, inaccessible repositories, and ambiguous zero flags can distort source sets. The high-impact-seeded sample limits npm-wide generalization.

\textbf{Internal and conclusion validity.} Independent traversals, dual incidence checks, toy cases, and generated values reduce implementation error. Frozen data inputs remain unchanged. Deterministic comparisons describe this snapshot; random orders quantify selection variability. Nested budgets and endpoint definitions reuse orders, so 6,000 budget rows are not independent samples.

\textbf{Boundaries and reporting.} Node.js's High-severity Brotli issue (CVE-2026-111497) illustrates native runtime dependencies outside npm manifests~\cite{nodejsbrotli2026}. Artifact integrity and source-tag identity do not establish equivalent or benign builds~\cite{litellmincident2026}. We execute no npm payloads or credential probes and claim no demonstrated compromise prevention. Prospective captures retain sanitized metadata and hashes without emails or package bodies.
