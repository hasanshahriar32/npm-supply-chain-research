\section{Threats to Validity and Data Handling}
Matching recomputation validates consistency with stored inputs and the formula, not collection completeness. Artifact differences could reflect collection or export timing, or another undocumented process; the audit does not establish a cause. Normalization, review budget, rounding, and tie policy affect queues. Finite-artifact totals do not justify ecosystem-wide inference or sampling confidence intervals.

Package projection removes version constraints and can join paths that no installation realized. Workflow directories do not establish actual dependency execution or privilege. Registry identities need not correspond one-to-one with publishers or organizations. High-impact sampling, shared repositories, campaign relatedness, and non-random missingness limit generalization.

The incident companion is inventoried rather than independently re-adjudicated. Recorded pre-cutoff publication times do not authenticate the original responses or guarantee immutability. Source deletion may depend on maliciousness, creating survivor bias; archive resources~\cite{pinckney2023} are not assumed to cover these events automatically. The targeted literature screen cannot establish exhaustive novelty.

The audit operates offline on supplied archives, accesses SQLite read-only, and reports aggregates. It does not acquire suspicious payloads, collect secrets, or label maintainers as malicious. Original artifacts are preserved; full provenance and the extended future protocol remain in author-side working materials. No model is fitted, and the proposed benchmark has not been evaluated.
