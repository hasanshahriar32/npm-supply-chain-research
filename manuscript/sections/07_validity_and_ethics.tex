\section{Validity, Limitations, and Responsible Reporting}
\textbf{Constructs.} Registry membership records a username association; it supplies no token scope, publishing-authority history, or evidence of independent account control. A graph path supplies no execution or secret-access evidence. The scenario prior, efficacy multiplier, and thresholds are assumptions; signer groups and bypass privileges were not observed.

\textbf{Observation and generalization.} Neither representation has a common field cutoff, workflow commits, or complete extraction-root provenance. Mixing resolution contexts can add paths, while missing metadata can omit source identities. The high-impact seed list also prevents treating these measurements as npm-wide estimates.

\textbf{Internal and conclusion validity.} Independent traversals and incidence checks test the implementation against the frozen data. They cannot repair missing observations. The 6,000 random rows reuse orders across nested budgets and endpoint definitions, so they are dependent comparisons rather than independent samples.

\textbf{Boundaries and reporting.} Runtime vulnerabilities such as the Node.js Brotli flaw (CVE-2026-111497) fall outside the npm manifest graph~\cite{nodejsbrotli2026}. The LiteLLM artifact mismatch also illustrates why a source tag alone cannot establish a released build's contents~\cite{litellmincident2026}. Our analysis executes no package payloads or credential probes. Prospective captures omit emails and package bodies; no prevention effect was measured.
