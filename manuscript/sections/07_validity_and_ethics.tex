\section{Validity, Limitations, and Responsible Reporting}
\textbf{Construct validity.} Observed maintainer membership does not establish active publishing authority, account independence, token scope, or control feasibility. Graph reach does not prove that a dependency is installed, executed in CI, or able to access secrets. Perfect source hardening and a uniform origin prior define a conditional comparison; they do not describe measured attack behavior. Real adversaries could adapt to controls.

\textbf{Observation validity.} Collection lacks a shared field-level cutoff, workflow commit identities, and extraction-root provenance. Even version continuity can mix resolution contexts. Missing metadata and inaccessible repositories can distort endpoints and source membership; zero workflow flags remain ambiguous. Our sensitivities describe observable changes rather than correcting every missing value. The high-impact-seeded sample limits ecosystem-wide generalization.

\textbf{Internal validity and reproducibility.} Independent traversals, dual incidence checks, toy cases, and generated tables reduce implementation error. Source archives remain unchanged. We recompute from graph relations and record earlier export inconsistencies as quality-control findings, rather than mixing incompatible scores. Deterministic graph measurements have no sampling confidence interval; random orders quantify baseline-selection variability only.

\textbf{Responsible reporting.} We report aggregates rather than individual maintainer priorities or executable attack instructions. We execute no npm payload, test no credentials, and claim no real-world compromise or prevention. Prospective captures retain sanitized metadata and response hashes without emails or package bodies. Scientific interpretations, sources, and disclosures require review by the human authors.
